# Obviously Awesome - April Dunford

> [!important]
> Core idea: Positioning is the act of deliberately defining:
>
> - For a **well defined market**,
> - How you are the **best**,
> - At something the market **cares about**.

Other interesting ideas:

- A good way to know your positioning is bad? Customers keep asking for features you don't plan on delivering.
  - They were probably expecting a different product.
- Customers will position you in their minds, whether you do it for them or not.
  - So help them position you in the right place.
- An interesting exercise:
  - Suppose your company is running out of cash, and you need an injection _right now_. What kind of customers will you go after?
- You can ask existing customers: Why did you try this? What made you stay?

The five components of effective positioning:

- Competitive Alternatives: What the customers would do if you didn't exist.
  - It's important to look beyond your direct competitors. This includes things like "Nothing", or "Use pen and paper".
  - For BT, this mostly includes things like YouTube, not just Syrinscape.
- Unique Attributes: What unique features do you have?
  - This, on its own, is pretty useless, but it'll inform the later decisions
- Value: How do those unique features translate into solutions to problems your users care about?
  - How can you prove that?
  - How can you offer a dollar for 10 cents?
- Target Market Characteristics
  - This is essentially your definition of an ICP, expressed more broadly.
  - Think about the best-fit customer vs all other customers. What makes them different?
- Market category: Like "databases", "data warehouses", "data warehouses for dentists"
  - This is fractal. a good strategy can be to carve a special niche in an existing market, to make sure you work for that niche.
  - Obviously, the niche has to be big enough.
  - The author gives an example where they had a CRM that was "a crappier version of the incumbent", but it had This One Feature for Investment Banks, and they just raised the prices and sold to them, to great success.
  - You can expand the market later.
  - April calls this the "Big Fish In Small Pond" tactic, which is usually better than a small fish in a pond with a big incumbent (which the author calls "Head to Head Style")

Then the author creates a step-by-step method to figure out positioning. I read it, but it's not as useful as the broad ideas.
