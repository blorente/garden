# Working in Public

### Chapter 2

- How a project grows. In general, the bigger the project, the bigger everything is, but not everything grows at the same rate. For instance, issues tend to grow more than commits, etc etc.
  - Not sure if this is in absolute numbers, I don't really trust the article cited, and I don't want to bother to check. I don't think it's useful to the point.
- It may be useful to look at oss projects in terms of two axes: Number of users relative to number of contributors:

|                   | High users            | Low Users                            |
| ----------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| High Contributors | Federation (eg. Rust) | Clubs (~every user is a contributor) |
| Low Contributors  | Stadiums (e.g. xz)    | Personal Projects, toys              |

- In general, this seems less useful as a way to classify projects, because the boundaries are fuzzy, but rather to reason about _how you want your project to behave_, and adjust your policies accordingly.
  - Federations need both wide, accessible paths to maintainerships, but also structured decision making process because there are so many maintainers.
  - Stadiums, on the other hand, run the risk of burnout, so they should focus more on just not crumbling under the weight.
  - Clubs are fun. I like clubs.
