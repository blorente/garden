# Hyperion - Dan Simmons

A _dense_ novel, one of those Sci-Fi novels that just assumes you know all the terminology of the new world from the start, and doesn't really bother explaining any of it until the plot demands that a character says it.

As a result, it took me like half a book to get used to the world.

Is that a bad thing? Not sure. Once I soldiered on, the world became honestly the best calling point for the book. It's evocative, so evocative, and the whole book is basically one big exploration of the future with the lenses of the different characters.

The plot in the book is _fine_ (a few travelers embark on a pilgrimage to Hyperion for the second time in their lives) but let's be honest -- the plot was just a vehicle to get interesting people telling their interesting stories. And those stories were only excuses to explore different aspects of the future Simmons imagined.

That's great! Evocative worldbuilding is like half the reason I like Sci-Fi, and this world is very interesting to explore. A+ for that.

To be honest, I'm not sure what my opinion is of the book. I finished it, but it took me 3 years. I loved the world, but I still can't quite picture some parts of it in my head, because of the made-up technobabble names.

I'll be reading the sequel though, so I guess it can't be that bad. And I'll be rooting for Rachel and Sol.
