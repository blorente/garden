# Book Notes - The Right It

## Data

- Do not rely on Other People's Data.
  - Especially if they are market studies based on asking people what they would do.
    - There are a lot of biases and errors that happen when asking.
    - Maybe they think they're going to use something, but they don't in reality.
    - Maybe they didn't quite understand what it was you were selling.
- Thoughtland is the enemy. The strongest guarantee you have is people actually buying something.
  - People _thinking_ they _might_ want to buy something is pretty weak.
- Get Your Own DatA (YODA).
  - YODA should be recent, verifiable, representative.

## Market Hypothesis

- A useful way to validate an idea is to form a market Hypothesis:
  > I believe that X% of \[population] will pay Y for Z

- This forces you to define a population, a price, and what it is you're offering.

- From there, we can design experiments, (pretotypes) because now we have something to measure.

- It's often useful to narrow down the population to a **representative** subset to make experiments more practical.
  - For instance, instead of 10% of all DMs, I could target 10% of the DMs that hang out in reddit.com/r/DungeonMasters.

## Pretotypes

A Pretotype is something you sell before you have anything to sell, just to see if people would buy it if you offered it.
Some relevant sorts of pretotypes:

- Mechanical Turk: If your solution promises to streamline/automate something, start by doing it yourself by hand and see if people buy it.
- Fake Door: Create a front for what you sell (a landing page) with nothing behind it, and see if people stop and look, and if they buy based on that.
  - You can even have pricing and collect emails.

## Validating

- You can iterate on your [[#Market Hypothesis]] as you gain insight. In fact, you might learn valuable stuff to tweak either the price, the population, or the product.
- You'll never know 100%, but it helps to rate the outcome of an experiment in one of 5 levels:
  - Wild success
  - Better than expected
  - As good as expected
  - Slightly below expectations
  - Dogshit

- Second day sushi is a perfect example of a Pretotype.
