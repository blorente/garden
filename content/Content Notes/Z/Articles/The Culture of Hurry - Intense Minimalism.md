# The culture of hurry

### Citing a paper:

> **A person in a hurry was 84% less likely to help** than one than one that wasn’t under any time pressure.

Is this true, does it have the right methodology?

- Subjects were seminary students, hopefully predisposed to help?
- They controlled for religiosity (whatever that means). It didn't affect things.
- 67 subjects at Princeton Theological Seminary, but only clean data from 40.
- They were paid some 16USD.
- Paper is from 1973.
- Not a huge sample, tremendously biased.

## Outcomes:

- Some orgs end up seeking efficiency for efficiency's sake, not because of a business goal.

Main problem of hurrying: Nobody is motivated to help, because everyone always has too many things to do.

Other problems:

- It's hard to change, because there's no wiggle room.
  - Or time to think about how to solve root causes.

> **The best approach to introduce any change in a hurry culture is to first remove the hurry**
